Chapter 1 — The Country

South Sudan: The World's Youngest Nation,
and Its Most Forgotten

The United Nations has named South Sudan the poorest country on earth. Behind that word — poorest — are millions of people, real communities, real ambitions, and real grief.

South Sudan gained independence in 2011 — the world's newest country. But independence did not bring peace. Years of civil war, tribal conflict, and economic collapse have left the country deeply fragile. Infrastructure barely exists. Hospitals are under-resourced. Schools lack teachers, desks, and books.

Women form the majority of the population — yet they carry a disproportionate share of the suffering. In many communities, girls are seen not as future leaders, doctors, or engineers, but as bargaining chips: married off young, often before they have even completed primary school.

The country sits landlocked in East-Central Africa, bordered by Sudan, Ethiopia, Kenya, Uganda, the Democratic Republic of Congo, and the Central African Republic. It is rich in oil — yet most of its people live without clean water, reliable electricity, or roads.

Chapter 2 — The Challenges

What These Young People Face
Every Day

Understanding PASS means first understanding what our scholars live through — before they ever sit down in a classroom.

💍
Early & Forced Marriage
In many communities across South Sudan, girls are married off as young as 12 or 13. This is not just tradition — it is an economic transaction. A daughter becomes a bride price. Education ends. Childhood ends. The cycle of poverty continues.
🔫
Armed Conflict & Insecurity
Tribal violence and political conflict have torn apart families and communities. Young men are recruited — sometimes by force — into armed groups. Young women face gender-based violence. Schools are burned, teachers flee, and education becomes impossible.
🏕️
Displacement & Refugee Life
Hundreds of thousands of South Sudanese have fled across borders. Our scholars are among them. Living in refugee camps or host communities in Kenya, Uganda, and Ethiopia — far from home, with uncertain futures. Yet still wanting to learn.
📵
No Internet. No Access. No Information.
Most of our scholars do not have reliable internet access. They cannot search for scholarships. They cannot apply online. They cannot discover what opportunities exist in the world for people like them. This isn't a gap — it's a wall. PASS tears it down.

PASS is not limited to South Sudan. Because the crisis is not contained within borders, neither are we. We actively support South Sudanese refugees across:

🇸🇸 South Sudan 🇰🇪 Kenya 🇺🇬 Uganda 🇪🇹 Ethiopia

Wherever our scholars are, we find them. We meet them where they are — because waiting for them to come to safety first would mean leaving them behind forever.

Chapter 4 — What PASS Does

Our Programs:
More Than Schoolbooks

Education without safety, confidence, and opportunity is incomplete. PASS builds all of it — together.

📚
Scholarships & School Access
We identify scholars without the means to pay school fees and connect them to funding — grants, bursaries, and donor support — that they could never find on their own, because they have no internet, no guide, and no network. We are their network.
SDG 4 · Quality Education
🥋
Self-Defense & Mental Resilience (ESD Global)
Adopted from ESD Global, our self-defense program is not just physical. It re-shapes the mind. For a girl who has survived gender-based violence, or a boy who has witnessed conflict, this program restores a sense of agency, safety, and self-worth. The body learns to stand firm — and so does the spirit.
Empowerment · Safety · ESD Global
💼
Career Development
A scholarship without a career path is a bridge that ends mid-air. We provide career guidance, mentorship, and exposure to professional pathways — helping scholars understand what they can become and how to get there, step by step.
SDG 4 · Decent Work & Growth
🤝
Mentorship & Community
Isolation is one of the greatest threats a scholar faces. We build communities — connecting scholars to mentors, to each other, and to PASS alumni who have walked the same path. You are not alone in this.
SDG 4 & 5 · Community
Why Boys Are Included — Even Though Women Come First

Our focus is firmly on women and girls, because they bear the greatest burden of inequality in South Sudan. But we do not exclude young men.

Here is why: the boy who grows up educated, mentored, and shown a different vision of manhood does not become the man who forces his daughter into marriage. He becomes a father who sends her to school.

Gender equality is not only a women's issue. It is a human project. Boys who are raised with dignity and purpose become allies, husbands, brothers, and fathers who change the cycle — not continue it.

Chapter 5 — Our Global Commitments

PASS and the
Sustainable Development Goals

PASS's work is grounded in two of the UN's Sustainable Development Goals. These are not just boxes we tick — they describe what we are building, every single day.

04
Quality Education
It's quality because it doesn't stop at the classroom door. PASS wraps education in mentorship, career guidance, and emotional support.
It's quality because we reach the unreached. Those without internet, without contacts, without means — they are our first priority.
It's quality because we follow the scholar. Wherever they are — in South Sudan, in a Kenyan camp, in Uganda — we do not abandon them.
It's quality because we think long-term. One educated generation changes the next. That is the investment we are making.
05
Gender Equality
It's equality because we challenge early marriage directly. We advocate for girls' right to stay in school, even when their communities push back.
It's equality because we equip women with self-defense. Physical safety and the ability to protect oneself is a foundation of freedom.
It's equality because we change minds around them. We include boys and young men so that the next generation normalizes equality — not just tolerates it.
It's equality because we follow displaced women. A refugee woman should not lose her right to education just because she crossed a border.

These goals are interconnected. A girl who is educated is less likely to be forced into early marriage. A woman who is safe and confident is more likely to stay in school. A community that values gender equality is one that invests in its own future. PASS lives at the intersection of both.
